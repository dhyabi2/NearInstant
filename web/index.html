The autonomous Hermes agent

          
This exchange runs as a skill for the Hermes agent — a self-hosted, self-improving AI agent. Once installed, Hermes makes the market for you on its own: it reads the live order book, prices offers with the same volatility-adaptive spread this page uses, posts, reprices and withdraws them on a schedule, and detects and certifies profitable takes. No browser, no babysitting — it drives the same protocol code the app does.


          
What Hermes does on its own

          
    
            
  • Reads the live order book and quotes a volatility-adaptive spread.
    • 
            
  • Posts, reprices and withdraws your offers on a schedule (cron).
    • 
            
  • Detects a taker, certifies the deal is a win, and declines the rest.
    • 
            
  • Runs unattended on a $5 VPS or your own machine; reports each cycle.
    • 
          


          
Certified win, or no action

          
Every offer, every accepted take, and every irreversible step is checked to be profitable at the live market — after fees, with active volatility monitoring and an unrealised-loss limit — or it is refused. It never acts on an unverified or fast-moving price.


          
Settlement is autonomous, end to end

          
Hermes settles each certified take itself — on by default: it runs the full 25–40 minute settlement (Monero confirmations included), and if the process dies partway it auto-resumes on the next tick, completing, refunding, or recovering. Every irreversible step is still certified against the live market, so a losing or fast-moving deal aborts safely. Prefer a person on the money? Set XNOXMR_AUTOSETTLE=0 and certified takes are handed to a human instead.


          
Run it fully self-hosted (local proof-of-work & nodes)

          
By default Hermes uses this site's proof-of-work helper and public RPC nodes. For full independence — no third party in the loop — point it at your own:

          
    
            
  • Nano proof-of-work is required for every block. Run a local worker and set XNOXMR_WORK_URL to it (otherwise Hermes falls back to slower in-process PoW).
    • 
            
  • Nano node — set XNOXMR_NANO_NODES to your own node(s).
    • 
            
  • Monero node — run your own monerod and set XNOXMR_MONERO_NODES to http://127.0.0.1:18081. Monero needs no client-side PoW; a local node just removes reliance on public ones.
    •

Why is joining early better?
One honest reason: less competition. Swappers always take the best-priced live offer; when few people are providing, more of the trades flow through your coins. There are no levels, ranks or priority — nothing is granted for tenure. It is not a guaranteed return: no trades, no earnings.

          
Can you take or freeze my coins?
No. This runs entirely in your browser with no server of ours in the middle. Your key is generated here, encrypted with your passphrase (memory-hard Argon2id → AES-256-GCM), and never leaves this device. A swap is enforced by cryptography, 2-of-2 joint accounts and adaptor signatures, not by trusting us or anyone else.

          
What if a swap fails, or I close the browser mid-swap?
Every completed step is saved on your device and no step can run twice. If anything fails mid-swap, recovery is automatic: reopen the page (wallet unlocked) and the swap under Unfinished swaps auto-resumes — it completes, takes the built-in refund so your XNO comes back, or reclaims your locked XMR from the counterparty’s refund. If the other side vanishes, your side refunds itself after a timeout. Still: keep the page open during a swap and use small amounts while this is young.

          
Do I need an account, email, or ID?
None. No sign-up, no KYC. You just choose a passphrase, which encrypts a wallet held only in this browser.

          
What are the fees?
We charge no platform fee. Nano is feeless; the only cost is Monero's small network fee. When you earn, you keep the spread the people trading through you pay.

          
How is the XNO/XMR price decided? Who sets it?
Nobody central. It's a peer-to-peer order book. When you provide, your offer is priced from the live market rate plus a spread that scales with volatility. When you swap, you pick the best offer that's open. The app may show the going market price from a reference exchange as a guide, but nobody is forced to use it, if an offer is priced badly, traders just pick another. That's what keeps it trustless: there is no rate-setter to trust.

          
How long does a swap take?
The Nano side is near-instant. The Monero side needs about 10 confirmations (~20 minutes) before it's spendable, that wait is handled automatically; you can close the page and it continues.

          
How is this different from an exchange?
An exchange holds your coins, it can freeze them, lose them, or demand your ID. Here nothing is ever custodied: the two coins settle directly against each other on-chain, so there's no company standing between you and your funds.

          
What does "earning" actually mean?
You offer just one coin (Nano or Monero) as liquidity. People swapping between the two trade through your offer and pay you a spread. You stay in your own coin, you only hold a little of the other coin briefly between trades. Earnings depend on real trading volume; the figures shown are honest estimates, not a promise.

          
How does a swap actually happen?
You take a live offer; your browser and the maker's co-sign the settlement over an encrypted relay, each holding only its own key share. Nobody — not us, not the other person — can move your coins without your half of the signature.

          
Can I recover my wallet on another device?
Yes — back up both. Back up seed restores everything in this app on any device. For Monero in a different wallet (Cake, Feather), use Back up Monero key: this app derives Monero keys from your seed differently, so the seed alone will not find your Monero there — the spend key will. If you clear this site's data with no backup, the wallet is gone for good. Nobody, including us, can recover it for you.

          
Is my Monero privacy fully protected?
Partly, and we want to be straight about the limit. The swap itself is non-custodial and the Monero side uses real ring signatures and decoys. But right now the swept Monero returns to your main wallet address, and it reuses that same address on every swap. An observer watching the public ledgers could link your swaps to each other and to your main Monero identity, which weakens Monero's usual privacy. Sending each swap to a fresh subaddress would fix this and is planned, but it isn't in yet. If unlinkability matters to you, forward the received Monero onward yourself for now, and prefer small amounts.

          
Is it audited? Should I use real money?
Honestly: not yet by an independent third party, and there is something more important to tell you. The cryptography is backed by adversarial tests, and an earlier single-operator version settled real funds on-chain — but the person-to-person swap you would use here has never completed on-chain between two browsers. You would be among the first. Treat this as an experiment, not a product, and only use what you would shrug off losing.

          
Which coins are supported?
Nano (XNO) and Monero (XMR) only, for now. More may be added later, the design is direction-agnostic.
